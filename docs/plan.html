Key decisions

Native SwiftUI, iOS 17.2+Live Activities, Dynamic Island and widgets are the core of the "sports app" feel. They need ActivityKit and WidgetKit, so the app is native Swift, not cross-platform.
Official sources firstHouse Clerk, Senate LIS, Congress.gov and GovInfo are free and authoritative. Third-party data covers two gaps only: district geocoding as a second option, and display-only market odds.
Always-on ingest workerVotes need polling every 5–15 seconds while a chamber is in session. Minute-level serverless crons are too slow, so a small long-running worker owns ingestion and push.
Postgres + realtime channelsOne Postgres database is the system of record. Live vote state reaches open apps over realtime channels and closed apps through APNs and Live Activity pushes.
AI analysis per bill, personalization per profileEach bill version is analyzed once, with section citations. The "for you" text combines that analysis with coarse profile attributes. No names or addresses go to the model.
Projections are ours; odds are display-onlyAn in-house model gives "% projected to pass" for every vote. Prediction-market prices appear only where a market exists, are labeled by source, and never link to trading.

What "live" can mean for each chamber

The v2 designs show a running 212–196 tally with a clock and member-by-member play-by-play. The government doesn't publish all of that in real time, so the app has to be designed around what actually exists.

Recommended stack

  • Language: TypeScript monorepo (worker, API, shared types). Swift models are hand-written against the committed OpenAPI contract and checked against it in CI (generation was tried in M2 and dropped; see HANDOFF.md).
  • Database, auth, realtime: Supabase. That's Postgres, Sign in with Apple, row-level security and realtime broadcast channels in one service.
  • Ingest worker: one small always-on container (Fly.io or Railway) running the pollers, OCR, the event bus and APNs sends.
  • Jobs: pg-boss queue inside Postgres, so AI and backfill jobs need no extra service.
  • Public API: Hono on Vercel (Node runtime), with CDN caching for bill and member pages.
  • Observability: Sentry (app + backend) and an alert whenever a source goes stale.

Why this split

  • Polling and push have to stay warm through a 15-minute vote. Request-scoped functions are the wrong shape for that.
  • Reads are huge and cacheable (bill pages, rep pages). Writes come from one place, the worker.
  • Each vendor can be swapped without touching the iOS app, because the app talks only to our API and realtime channel.

Live vote pipeline

Session detectionThe worker reads the daily schedule and floor summary. It polls every 10 s while a chamber is in session and backs off to every 5 min otherwise.
Vote opensA floor event says a recorded vote has started. The worker creates a vote row with state open, links it to the bill or amendment, and records the vote type and threshold (majority, 3/5 cloture, 2/3 suspension).
Fan-outA realtime broadcast goes to open apps. Followers of the bill or of any rep in the chamber get an APNs push that starts a Live Activity (push-to-start, iOS 17.2+). Everyone else who opted in gets a normal notification.
During the voteHouse: the OCR tally updates the score and clock every ~5 s, and each update is pushed to the Live Activity. Both chambers: the projection recomputes on each update or new statement.
Vote closesThe worker polls for the roll call XML every 10 s until it appears. It then parses member positions, checks them against the OCR totals (and flags any mismatch), and sets state final.
Personal resultsEach user gets "Your rep voted Yea · it passed 219–208" with a one-line "for you" summary. The Live Activity ends with a final state and stays on the lock screen for up to 4 hours.

Freshness targets: floor event to push under 30 s; roll call posted to "your rep voted" under 60 s; OCR tally to Live Activity under 10 s.

AI: bill impact and "What it means for you"

Layer 1 · per bill version (shared)

  • Inputs: bill text (GovInfo), CRS summary, CBO estimate, committee report.
  • Output, as structured JSON: purpose, 3–5 key provisions, cost, affected groups with direction (▲ ▼ ●), supporter and opponent arguments, and impact rules keyed by profile attributes. Every claim cites a bill section.
  • Models: Claude Sonnet 5.5 for analysis, Claude Haiku 5.5 for tagging and citation checks.
  • Runs once per text version, so cost scales with bills, not users.

Layer 2 · per user (personal)

  • Profile is coarse and opt-in: state, district, county, and optionally renter or owner, income band, veteran, student, parent, age band, work sector.
  • The impact rules are matched to the profile, then filled with county statistics (ACS, FCC). A short model call writes the paragraph. Results are cached by profile bucket, so many users share one result.
  • The model never sees names, emails or street addresses. The address is used once to find the district, then discarded.

Guardrails: a neutrality rubric in the prompt, a second automated pass that checks each claim against its cited section, a fixed eval set of ~50 bills with human-written reference summaries, human review for the top 20 bills each week, an "AI summary · may contain errors" label, a link to the source text, and the thumbs-up/down feedback already in the design.

Projection model ("% projected to pass")

  • Pre-vote: per-member probability of Yea from party and leadership position, cosponsorship, committee vote, vote on the rule or procedural motion, ideological distance (DW-NOMINATE) from the sponsors, past votes on similar bills, and announced positions.
  • Aggregate: simulate the chamber (Monte Carlo), apply the vote's threshold, and report a pass probability plus a projected final tally.
  • Live: update on each running total (House OCR) and on new statements. Members already counted drop out of the uncertainty.
  • Validation: backtest on all 118th and 119th Congress roll calls, publish a calibration chart on an in-app "How projections work" page, and beat a party-line baseline on Brier score.
  • Market odds: when a Kalshi or Polymarket market clearly matches the same question, show its price as a secondary line ("Markets: 77%") with the source named. It's display-only: no links to trade and no betting language.

iOS app

Frameworks

  • SwiftUI + Observation and Swift 6 concurrency, with a minimum of iOS 17.2 for push-to-start Live Activities.
  • ActivityKit (Live Activity + Dynamic Island), WidgetKit (score, "your reps today"), App Intents (follow and unfollow from widgets).
  • Swift Charts for the projection line and coalition bars.
  • SwiftData for an offline cache of followed bills, reps and recent votes.
  • Supabase Swift SDK for auth and realtime. URLSession for the REST API.
  • Sign in with Apple. Location is optional and only used to suggest a district.

Structure

  • Swift packages (one FloorKit package, one library per area): Models (checked against the OpenAPI contract), APIClient, DesignSystem (scoreboard cards, crests, bars), and one per feature — built: Onboarding, Home, VoteScreen, BillScreen, RepScreen; still to come: Realtime, Alerts.
  • The widget and Live Activity extensions share Models and DesignSystem through an App Group.
  • Notification categories: vote opened, rep voted, bill passed or failed, scheduled vote reminder.
  • Navigation (v0.2.3): three tabs (Finals, Bills, Your three), each with its own stack. Display type is Barlow Condensed (OFL, bundled); colours stay in Palette. The screens follow the locked phone mocks in the design canvas.
  • Appearance (v0.2.5): the app follows the system light or dark setting. Dark is the canvas palette; light is “Marble & Patina” (cool marble ground, white panels, patina accent). The split-flap board and the turf scoreboard stay dark in both, as lit displays.
  • Tests: Swift Testing for models and parsers, snapshot tests for every card state (open, live tally, pending, final), and UI tests for onboarding.

Android comes later as a native Kotlin + Jetpack Compose app on the same API. The only iOS-specific part of the backend is the APNs sender.

Milestones

M0

Foundation and test harness

Monorepo, Xcode project, CI (build, lint, unit and snapshot tests), Supabase project and schema migrations, an API skeleton with OpenAPI, and recorded copies of real Clerk, Senate and Congress.gov payloads for parser tests.

Exit: CI green on every push; the app runs on the simulator against a staging API.

M1

Data backbone

Ingest members, bills, actions, summaries, nominations, and House and Senate roll calls (backfilled to 2023). Add district lookup and freshness monitoring.

Exit: any vote from 2023 on has correct member positions; address → district → reps works.

M2

iOS core on real data

Onboarding (address or location to your reps), Home, Vote detail, Bill and Rep screens built from the v2 designs, showing final results only (no live yet).

Exit: a TestFlight build a tester can use to see how their reps voted yesterday.

M3

Live layer

Session-aware floor polling, vote open/close events, realtime channels, APNs, Live Activities and widgets. Measure real Clerk and LIS roll call posting latency, which needs polling running on a live session day. Prototype the House tally OCR and decide go/no-go on it.

Exit: during a real House vote, the phone shows "vote open" in under 30 s and your rep's vote in under 60 s after the roll call posts.

M4

Understanding: AI + projections

Layer 1 and Layer 2 AI analysis with citations and the eval set. Projection model v1 with backtest and calibration page. Optional market odds line.

Exit: the eval set passes the factuality bar; projections beat the party-line baseline on backtest.

M5

Beta hardening and App Store

Accessibility (Dynamic Type, VoiceOver on score cards), privacy labels, a load test for vote-night spikes, App Review prep, public TestFlight. The web landing page was built early, in v0.2.0 (apps/web).

Exit: submitted to App Review.

v0.3.0

Your members on the web

In progress, alongside M2. Location → your members on the landing page; live finals, scouting report and player cards from the API; campaign finance for every member from OpenFEC; donor context from Senate LDA lobbying filings; a financial-disclosure spike (PDF parsing and the 5 U.S.C. §13107 commercial-use question) before any build.

Exit: the landing page shows the visitor's own three members, with real data or a visible placeholder in every section.
